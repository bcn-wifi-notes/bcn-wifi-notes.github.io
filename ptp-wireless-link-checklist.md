# Checklist: data you need before a point-to-point wireless link

**QUICK SUMMARY**  
Before anyone aims two radios at each other, write down the problem. Two buildings need a private path. Fibre is late, expensive, or missing. A point-to-point wireless link can work, but only if the path and the traffic are understood first. This page is a practical checklist of what to gather before you request a design or a quote.

## Why a checklist first

Buying hardware without a path study is a common reason links underperform. Distance on a map is not the same as clear radio path. Capacity for file sync is not the same as capacity for continuous CCTV. Support after install is not the same as a one-day mount.

Office WiFi and inter-building links are different jobs. Indoor coverage does not replace a site-to-site link, and a long-range link does not replace floor WiFi.

## Data to collect before a proposal

1. **Addresses of both ends** and approximate distance (map is fine; field survey confirms).
2. **Roof or mast access**, mounting height, and who must approve installation.
3. **Line-of-sight notes** toward the other site: trees, cranes, new buildings, hills, seasonal foliage.
4. **Required capacity now** and a realistic 12–24 month view (including any video or voice).
5. **How critical the link is** — best-effort vs dual radio with automatic failover.
6. **Power** at each mount point and cable route into the network rack.
7. **Who monitors the link** after handover and how faults are reported.
8. **Landlord or safety constraints** (roof rules, working at height, permissions).

## Line of sight is not brochure text

Almost every serious point-to-point link needs a clear or near-clear path. Catalogue ranges mean little if the Fresnel zone is blocked. Ask for a real path assessment for your two locations, not a generic “up to X km” claim with no site context.

## Realistic performance (order of magnitude)

In professional projects, designs often target up to about **1 Gbps**. Under good conditions, **gigabit-class** performance is typical on shorter clear paths (on the order of up to about **5 km**), and on the order of up to about **500 Mbps** toward about **10 km**, always depending on radios, antenna height, and path quality. Multiple links can be aggregated when the design allows. Treat every figure as conditional on the survey.

A standard straightforward install is often measured in about **one working day** after path analysis; access and permissions can extend that.

## Security and operations

Traffic between sites should be encrypted end to end. Agree who holds management access, how the link sits next to guest WiFi or cameras, and whether maintenance (alignment, firmware, weather checks) is included.

## When fibre is still the better answer

If fibre is available at a reasonable cost and timeline, use it. Wireless is the practical primary path when civil works, lead time, or temporary sites make fibre unrealistic—not a fashion choice.
